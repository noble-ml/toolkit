Licences and provenance

Publicly visible images are not automatically free to reuse. Felix records what each provider says about an image, checks it against how you release the dataset, and writes the files that go with it. This is guidance from the licence names providers report, not legal advice: check the terms before publishing or using a dataset commercially.

What every image records

Toddler words: every picture carries a little note: where it came from, who made it, and the rules for using it.

Its annotation file holds a source block, for example:

source:
  type: wikimedia
  title: "File:Sawtooth Oak Leaves.jpg"
  url: https://upload.wikimedia.org/…/1024px-….jpg
  page: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:…
  license: CC BY-SA 3.0
  license_url: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0
  author: Derek Ramsey
  attribution: "Sawtooth Oak Leaves.jpg by Derek Ramsey, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons (https://…)"
  query: plant leaves
  acquired: "2026-11-21T09:15:02Z"

Depending on the source: original URL and file name, dataset id, licence and its URL, author and their page, attribution text, the provider's usage terms, acquisition time, and for video frames the video file, frame number and time. Transforms and annotation add history; face blurring adds privacy.

Open or closed

Toddler words: decide whether everyone may use your dataset (open) or only you (closed); Felix checks every picture's rules against that.

fx.export("leaves.felix")                          # closed (default)
fx.export("leaves.felix", license="CC-BY-4.0")     # open, under this licence
fx.export("leaves.felix", license="auto")          # open, under the most permissive licence every image allows
fx.export("leaves.felix", license="CC-BY-4.0", strict=True)   # leave out images that do not fit
  • Closed: the compilation and annotations are all rights reserved (LICENSE.txt says so, naming authors as the owner). Third-party images keep their licences and are still credited. Felix flags images with unknown licences, all-rights-reserved images, non-commercial images (in case you use the data commercially) and no-derivatives images that were cropped.
  • Open: everything above, plus conflicts with the chosen licence: non-commercial images under a licence that allows commercial use, share-alike images under a licence without share-alike, attribution-required images under CC0, your own files (confirm you hold the rights and people's consent), and photos whose licence does not clearly allow redistribution in a dataset (Unsplash, Pexels). "auto" picks CC0 if every image is free of conditions, CC BY 4.0 if some need credit, CC BY-SA 4.0 with share-alike images, and the NC variants if any image is non-commercial.

Problems never stop the export (unless strict=True leaves those images out). They are listed in ATTRIBUTIONS.md (marked ⚠) and counted in metadata.yml under license.

The files

Toddler words: three notes that travel in the box: the rules for the whole dataset, a thank-you list for every photographer, and how to cite it.

unzip -p leaves.felix ATTRIBUTIONS.md | head -20      # read the credits inside a .felix file
unzip -p leaves.felix LICENSE.txt
FileContents
LICENSE.txtthe dataset's licence (or the closed notice), that images keep their own licences, and a count per image licence
ATTRIBUTIONS.mdevery image: file, source, licence (with any ⚠ issue), author / credit, link
CITATION.cffhow to cite the dataset (title, authors, date, licence), plus the datasets and collections it draws on (Open Images, COCO, Wikimedia Commons, iNaturalist, …)

They sit next to the data: inside a .felix file, at the top of a dataset folder, or beside the shards.

Faces

Toddler words: put a fuzzy sticker over people's faces, so nobody can be recognised.

fx.acquire([fx.sources.local("./street-photos")], blur_faces=True)   # blur before anything else
fx.transform.blur_faces(strength=1.0, min_size=20)                    # or as a step of its own

fx.transform.blur_faces() (or fx.acquire(..., blur_faces=True)) blurs faces found by OpenCV's frontal and profile face detectors, and records how many in each image. It is best effort: small, turned, partly hidden or unusual faces can be missed. Do not rely on it alone for sensitive data.
