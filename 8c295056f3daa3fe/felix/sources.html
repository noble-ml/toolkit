Sources

Pass any mix to fx.acquire([...]). Every image keeps a source record in its annotation: where it came from, its licence, who to credit and when it was collected. limit=None (the default for online sources) means "as many as the provider gives": with fx.export(..., pairs=N) the export simply stops asking once it has N pairs.

Several searches. Every search argument also takes a list: query=[...] (Wikimedia, Openverse, Flickr, Unsplash, Pexels, NASA, the Art Institute, the Smithsonian), taxon=[...] (iNaturalist), scientific_name=[...] (GBIF). The searches run side by side, share limit equally (limit=900 with three queries: 300 each), and a picture found by two is taken once. Each picture's source records the search that found it.

No target, no problem. The query, taxon or class of the search sources is optional: fx.sources.wikimedia() gives random Commons files, openverse() / nasa() / smithsonian() / gbif() / inaturalist() whatever they list, unsplash() the newest photos, pexels() the curated ones, flickr() recent uploads (licence-filtered), and open_images() the classes of your classes.yml (all 600 box classes without one). Pair it with fx.annotate to label what comes in.

A source with no "anything" feed of its own (the Art Institute), or whose feed runs dry (Openverse stops after 240), goes on with a vocabulary: by default about 4,600 subjects, the Recognize Anything (RAM++) tag list (Apache-2.0, bundled) plus everyday subjects, asked in shuffled order, at most 25 pictures each. Choose another with .using(vocabulary="general") (~150 everyday subjects), "ram++", or your own list, and per_term= for the number of pictures per subject. Pictures found by two subjects are taken once.

Online sources send a User-Agent naming Felix. Set FELIX_CONTACT (an email or URL) or contact: in ~/.config/felix/credentials.yml to include a way to reach you. Wikimedia requires it: without it, its image server refuses Felix, and Felix stops with a message saying so.

Speed

  • Parallel downloads. Each source downloads up to 16 images at once, over kept-alive connections (no new handshake per image). Image hosts built for it (S3 buckets, CDNs) take up to 16 at a time; others take 2–4. Different sources run side by side.
  • Polite APIs. Search APIs are still asked one page at a time, spaced as their rules ask (iNaturalist about one request a second, Pexels 200 an hour, …).
  • Slow-downs. A server that answers "too many requests" (429 / 503) pauses only that server, for as long as it asks; everything else carries on.
  • Download cache. Downloaded images are kept in ~/.cache/felix/images (most recently used first, up to FELIX_IMAGE_CACHE_GB, default 10). Re-running an export after changing a transform or the model needs no network. FELIX_IMAGE_CACHE=0 turns it off.
  • Limits are met. A failed download frees its place, so limit= and pairs= still get their full count.

Measured on a home connection, 48 images, cache off: Open Images 0.5 → 9.4 img/s (Felix 1.1 → 1.2); iNaturalist 0.3 → 6.2 img/s; a cached re-run 40–60 img/s.

On this computer

fx.sources.local(path, recursive=True, labels=None, license=None)

  • An image file, a folder of images (any nesting; recursive=False for the top level only) or an archive (.zip, .tar, .tar.gz, …, unpacked safely into a working folder).
  • Existing datasets are recognised and bring their labels and splits: Felix (.felix, folders, .sh_felix shards), COCO (any *.json with images / annotations / categories, e.g. Roboflow's _annotations.coco.json in train/ valid/ test/), YOLO (data.yaml with names and images/ + labels/).
  • labels="folders": a folder of class folders (cat/, dog/, …) becomes image labels (classification).
  • Photos stored sideways by a camera (EXIF rotation) are turned upright.
  • license: the images' licence if you know it; otherwise they are recorded as your own files.

fx.sources.video(path, sampling=fx.sampling.auto(), license=None, attribution=None)

Frames of a video file or a video URL (OpenCV: pip install opencv-python). Each frame records the video, frame number and time. A local file counts as your own recording; for a URL, say its license (otherwise it is unknown, which the default use="commercial" leaves out). Downloaded videos are cached. See Sampling.

fx.sources.camera(device=0, sampling=fx.sampling.interval(seconds=1), count=None, duration=None)

A webcam / USB camera (device=0, 1, …) or a stream URL ("rtsp://…", "http://…/video.mjpg"). Runs until count frames, duration seconds, the export's pairs, or Ctrl+C. random sampling is not available for live cameras.

Sampling

  • fx.sampling.auto(threshold=0.04, min_gap=0.5, max_gap=None, check_fps=4, limit=None): keep a frame when it differs from the last kept one by at least threshold (mean colour difference of small thumbnails), at least min_gap seconds apart. A still scene gives one frame, not hundreds. max_gap forces a frame every so often.
  • fx.sampling.interval(seconds=2, start=0, limit=None): one frame every seconds.
  • fx.sampling.random(count=1000, seed=42): count frames spread at random over the whole video, reproducibly.

On the web

fx.sources.urls(source, license=None, attribution=None)

One URL, a list, or a text file with one URL per line (# comments allowed). Their licences are unknown unless you say (license=, attribution=); export then reminds you to check your rights.

fx.sources.wikimedia(query=None, limit=None, licenses=None, max_side=2048, min_side=256)

Wikimedia Commons (MediaWiki API, File namespace, bitmap images). Records licence, author, credit and the file page, and builds the attribution line. licenses=["CC0", "Public domain", "CC BY 4.0"] keeps only those (by the start of Commons' short licence name). Downloads a scaled copy up to max_side, not 50-megapixel originals. No query: random files.

fx.sources.openverse(query=None, limit=None, licenses=None, commercial=True, source=None)

Openverse: openly licensed images from Flickr, Wikimedia and many museums, in one search (no key). commercial=True keeps licences that allow commercial use; licenses=["cc0", "by", "by-sa"] to choose; source="flickr" for one provider. Openverse supplies the attribution text.

fx.sources.flickr(query=None, limit=None, licenses=(4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12), api_key=None)

Flickr search filtered by licence id (default: CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0, public domain and "no known copyright", i.e. commercial use and changes allowed). Flickr's API is for non-commercial use unless you arrange otherwise with Flickr, and returns at most 4,000 results per search. No query: recent uploads with those licences.

fx.sources.unsplash(query=None, limit=None, size="regular", orientation=None, api_key=None)

Unsplash (Unsplash License). As Unsplash's API guidelines require, each download is reported to Unsplash. Demo apps are limited to 50 requests an hour. No query: the newest photos.

fx.sources.pexels(query=None, limit=None, size="large2x", orientation=None, api_key=None)

Pexels (Pexels License; 200 requests an hour, so Felix waits ~18 s between result pages). The "Photo by … on Pexels" credit is recorded. No query: Pexels' curated photos.

Unsplash and Pexels photos are free to use, but both licences forbid compiling their photos into a similar or competing service; export flags them when you release a dataset openly.

Videos on the web

Each of these searches a video site, downloads the videos whose licence fits fx.export(use=...) (the others are skipped before downloading) and samples them into frames like fx.sources.video. Shared options:

  • limit: frames in total; per_video=20: at most this many frames from one video, spread over its whole length;
  • sampling: fx.sampling.auto() (default: only when the scene changes), interval(seconds=) or random(count=);
  • max_height=720: the version to download (the tallest one no taller than this); max_seconds=600: skip longer videos (when the site says how long they are); max_mb=150: skip bigger files (checked before downloading); a download slower than 3 minutes is skipped too, so one huge file never holds the source up.

Videos are cached in ~/.cache/felix/videos (FELIX_VIDEO_CACHE_GB, default 20): a re-run downloads nothing.

fx.sources.pexels_videos(query=None, limit=None, sampling=None, per_video=20, max_seconds=600, max_height=720, orientation=None, api_key=None)

Stock footage from Pexels, with the same key as fx.sources.pexels. Without a query: popular videos. Pexels License (free, commercial use allowed); the credit "Video by … on Pexels" is recorded.

fx.sources.pixabay_videos(query=None, limit=None, sampling=None, per_video=20, max_seconds=600, max_height=720, api_key=None)

Stock footage from Pixabay (key: PIXABAY_API_KEY). Without a query: popular videos. Pixabay Content License (free, commercial use allowed, no credit required; it is recorded anyway). The API pages through at most 500 videos per search.

fx.sources.wikimedia_videos(query=None, limit=None, sampling=None, per_video=20, max_seconds=600, max_height=720)

Videos from Wikimedia Commons, using Commons' own WebM versions. Freely licensed, but many are share-alike (left out by the default use="commercial"; use="open" takes them). Set FELIX_CONTACT first, as for fx.sources.wikimedia. Without a query: Felix's general vocabulary, a few videos per subject.

fx.sources.nasa_videos(query=None, limit=None, sampling=None, per_video=20, max_seconds=600, max_height=720)

Videos from the NASA Image and Video Library: generally not copyrighted in the US (some items are third-party; see NASA's media guidelines). NASA doesn't say how long a video is, so max_mb is what keeps long ones out.

fx.sources.archive_videos(query=None, limit=None, sampling=None, per_video=20, max_seconds=600, max_height=720, max_mb=150)

Videos from the Internet Archive that state a licence (public domain or Creative Commons), most downloaded first; its MPEG-4 versions are used. The query is Internet Archive search syntax ("delivery truck", title:(parcel) AND year:[1950 TO 1970]). Its servers can be slow: slow downloads are skipped and reported.

Open collections

fx.sources.inaturalist(taxon=None, limit=None, licenses=("cc0", "cc-by", "cc-by-sa"), size="large", quality="research", label=False, place_id=None)

Research-grade iNaturalist observation photos for a taxon (scientific or common name), with each photo's own licence and attribution. label=True labels each image with its species (classification). Pages stop at 10,000 results. No taxon: any species. The name is looked up first and means exactly that taxon and everything under it ("Pinus": all pines, not the pine siskin *Spinus pinus*; common names work: "red oak" is *Quercus rubra*).

fx.sources.gbif(scientific_name=None, query=None, limit=None, licenses=("CC0_1_0", "CC_BY_4_0"), country=None)

Occurrence images from GBIF, with the publishing institution's licence and rights holder. Neither name nor query: any occurrence with an image.

fx.sources.nasa(query=None, limit=None, size="large")

The NASA Image and Video Library. NASA imagery is generally not copyrighted in the US, but some items are third-party or show people and logos: check NASA's media guidelines.

fx.sources.artic(query=None, limit=None) · fx.sources.smithsonian(query=None, limit=None, api_key=None)

Public-domain (CC0) images from the Art Institute of Chicago (843 px IIIF images, as the museum asks) and the Smithsonian's Open Access. The Art Institute's API sits behind a bot check that sometimes refuses programs for a while; Felix then says so instead of failing silently.

Not included, on purpose: Pixabay (its terms forbid automated collection and machine-learning use without permission), The Met and the Library of Congress (their servers only serve browsers: automated requests get a bot check, and Felix does not work around those).

Dataset hubs

fx.sources.roboflow(project, version, workspace=None, api_key=None)

A Roboflow dataset version (your workspace or Universe: project="workspace/project"), downloaded as a COCO export and imported with boxes and splits. Cached in ~/.cache/felix/roboflow. A Universe project without a licence means all rights reserved.

fx.sources.open_images(classes=None, split="validation", limit=None, seed=0, group_of=True)

Open Images V7 images containing any of classes (display names: "Car", "Person", …), with all their boxes of those classes. Without classes: your classes.yml's classes that Open Images has (names or aliases), or all 600 box classes. The annotation CSVs are cached in ~/.cache/felix/open_images (validation ~40 MB, test ~120 MB, train several GB). Each image keeps its own licence, author and Flickr page; annotations are CC BY 4.0 (Google). Images that Open Images lists as rotated are skipped, as their boxes may not line up.

fx.sources.coco(split="val2017", limit=None)

COCO 2017 from the official files (val2017: 5k images, ~1 GB with annotations; train2017: 118k images, ~18 GB), cached in ~/.cache/felix/coco. With a class file only images containing those classes are kept. Each image keeps its Flickr licence from COCO's metadata.

fx.sources.huggingface(dataset, split="train", config=None, limit=None, label_column=None, bbox_format="xywh", token=None)

A Hugging Face image dataset through the Dataset Viewer API (only the rows you take are downloaded). A class-label column becomes the image label; an objects column with bbox and category gives boxes. The dataset card's licence is recorded.

fx.sources.kaggle(dataset, labels=None)

A Kaggle dataset ("owner/name"), downloaded and imported like local (format detected automatically).

fx.sources.mapillary(bbox, limit=None, access_token=None)

Street-level images inside a map area smaller than 0.01° on each side (Mapillary's limit), CC BY-SA 4.0.

Your own source

Subclass fx.sources.Source with collect(self, collector, progress) and call collector.add(path=... or data=..., name=..., source={...}, objects=None, label=None) for each image.
